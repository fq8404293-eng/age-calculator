What Is Engine Compression Ratio?

Engine compression ratio describes the relationship between the total volume inside a cylinder when the piston is at bottom dead center and the volume remaining when the piston reaches top dead center.

For example, a compression ratio of 10:1 means the cylinder's total volume at bottom dead center is ten times the clearance volume remaining at top dead center.

Compression ratio is an important engine specification because it influences combustion characteristics, efficiency, performance and the fuel requirements of an engine.

Compression Ratio Formula

The static compression ratio is calculated using:

Compression Ratio = (Swept Volume + Clearance Volume) ÷ Clearance Volume

Swept Volume

Swept volume is the volume displaced by the piston as it moves from bottom dead center to top dead center.

Clearance Volume

Clearance volume is the volume remaining in the cylinder above the piston when the piston is at top dead center.

Total Cylinder Volume

Total cylinder volume is the sum of swept volume and clearance volume.

Compression Ratio Example

Suppose an engine has a swept volume of 500 cc and a clearance volume of 50 cc.

Total cylinder volume = 500 + 50 = 550 cc.

Compression ratio = 550 ÷ 50 = 11:1.

This means the total cylinder volume is eleven times the clearance volume.

Static vs. Dynamic Compression Ratio

This calculator estimates static compression ratio. It uses the full swept volume and clearance volume and does not account for the timing of the intake valve.

Dynamic compression ratio is a different measurement that considers when the intake valve actually closes and therefore uses a different effective compression calculation.

For engine specifications and basic comparisons, static compression ratio is commonly reported as a simple ratio such as 9:1, 10:1 or 12:1.

Why Compression Ratio Matters

Compression ratio affects how much the air-fuel mixture is compressed before combustion. Changes in compression ratio can influence engine efficiency, power characteristics, combustion temperature and fuel requirements.

A higher compression ratio does not automatically mean an engine is better for every application. Engine design, combustion chamber shape, ignition timing, fuel octane, forced induction and many other factors also affect performance.

When modifying an engine, use manufacturer specifications and appropriate engineering measurements rather than relying on compression ratio alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a compression ratio of 10:1?

A 10:1 compression ratio means the total cylinder volume at bottom dead center is ten times the clearance volume at top dead center.

What is swept volume?

Swept volume is the volume displaced by the piston as it travels through its stroke from bottom dead center to top dead center.

What is clearance volume?

Clearance volume is the space remaining above the piston when the piston reaches top dead center.

Does engine displacement equal swept volume?

For a single cylinder, swept volume is that cylinder's displacement. Total engine displacement is the combined swept volume of all cylinders.

Does this calculator calculate dynamic compression ratio?

No. This calculator calculates static compression ratio using swept volume and clearance volume. Dynamic compression requires additional information such as intake valve closing timing.

Can I use cc, liters or cubic inches?

Yes. The calculator supports cubic centimeters, liters and cubic inches. The swept volume and clearance volume must use the same unit.
