What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on an original amount together with interest that has accumulated previously. As the balance grows, future interest can be calculated on that larger balance.

This is different from a calculation where interest is always based only on the original principal. Over longer periods, the difference between these approaches can become significant.

A Simple Example

Suppose you start with $10,000 and the assumed annual interest rate is 7%. If the interest is compounded, interest earned during one period can become part of the balance used for later calculations.

If you also add money regularly, those additional contributions can increase the amount available to earn future interest.

Compound Interest Formula

A commonly used compound interest formula for a single initial investment is:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

In this formula:

  • A = final amount
  • P = original principal
  • r = annual interest rate expressed as a decimal
  • n = number of compounding periods per year
  • t = investment period in years

When regular contributions are included, the calculation becomes more involved because each contribution can have a different amount of time to grow. This calculator accounts for the monthly contribution separately.

Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

The main difference is how previously earned interest is treated.

Feature Simple Interest Compound Interest
Interest calculated on Original principal Principal plus accumulated interest
Previous interest can earn interest No Yes
Effect of longer periods More linear Can become increasingly significant
Regular contributions Depend on the product or calculation Can be included in an investment-growth calculation

What Does Compounding Frequency Mean?

Compounding frequency describes how often interest is added to the balance. Common choices include annual, semi-annual, quarterly, monthly and daily compounding.

When all other assumptions remain the same, changing the compounding frequency can change the calculated final amount. The actual terms of a savings account, investment or financial product should always be checked separately.

Common Compounding Frequencies

  • Annually: once per year
  • Semi-annually: twice per year
  • Quarterly: four times per year
  • Monthly: twelve times per year
  • Daily: approximately 365 times per year

How Monthly Contributions Affect Growth

Starting with a lump sum is only one way to build an investment balance. Regular contributions can add new money throughout the investment period.

In this calculator, the monthly contribution is added throughout the selected investment period. Because each contribution is made at a different point in time, earlier contributions generally have more time to participate in future growth than later contributions.

You can set the monthly contribution to zero if you only want to estimate the growth of the initial investment.

Why Does Time Matter?

Time can have an important effect on compound-growth calculations. When interest is added to a balance, subsequent calculations can use the increased balance.

Extending an investment period also gives existing contributions more time to potentially earn additional interest. This is one reason long-term calculations can look very different from short-term calculations even when the assumed annual rate stays the same.

The calculator lets you experiment with different periods so you can see how changing the time assumption affects the estimated result.

Common Mistakes When Estimating Compound Growth

Assuming the rate is guaranteed

An assumed rate in a calculator is not necessarily the rate an investment will actually earn.

Ignoring fees and taxes

Real financial products can involve fees, taxes or other costs that are not included in a basic compound-growth calculation.

Forgetting the effect of time

A small change in the investment period can materially change a long-term estimate because contributions and accumulated interest have different amounts of time to grow.

Treating the result as a guarantee

A calculator provides a mathematical estimate based on assumptions. It does not guarantee a particular investment outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on an original amount together with interest that has accumulated previously. As the balance changes, future interest can be calculated on the larger amount.

How does compound interest work?

Interest is added to the balance according to the terms and compounding frequency being used. Once added, that interest can become part of the balance used for future calculations.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is generally calculated using the original principal, while compound interest can include previously accumulated interest in later calculations.

What does compounding frequency mean?

Compounding frequency is how often interest is added to the balance. Examples include annually, quarterly, monthly and daily.

Can I include monthly contributions?

Yes. Enter your expected monthly contribution in the calculator. The calculator will include those regular additions when estimating the future value.

Does compound interest guarantee investment returns?

No. The calculator uses the rate you enter as an assumption. Actual returns can be different and may change over time.

Why can actual results differ from the calculator?

Actual results can differ because of changing rates, investment performance, fees, taxes, deposits, withdrawals and other factors.

Why does the investment period matter?

A longer period gives accumulated balances and contributions more time to participate in future calculations. This can make the difference between shorter and longer estimates substantial.

Can I use this calculator for savings accounts?

Yes. You can use it for an estimate when the account's interest rate and compounding terms can reasonably be represented by the calculator's inputs.

Can I use this calculator for investments?

You can use it to explore hypothetical growth assumptions, but actual investment returns are not guaranteed and can vary over time.

Does this calculator include taxes and fees?

No. Unless specifically stated otherwise, the calculation does not account for taxes, account fees, management fees or other investment costs.

What happens if I only make monthly contributions?

You can enter zero as the initial investment and enter your regular monthly contribution. The calculator can then estimate growth based on those contributions and the assumed interest rate.

Important Information

This calculator is provided for educational and planning purposes. The result is an estimate based on the assumptions you enter and should not be considered a guarantee of future investment performance.

Actual financial results can be affected by interest-rate changes, market performance, fees, taxes, inflation, deposits, withdrawals and other factors.
