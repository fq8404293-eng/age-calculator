What Is a Hexadecimal Number?

Hexadecimal is a base-16 number system that uses sixteen symbols to represent values. It uses the digits 0 through 9 and the letters A through F.

The letters represent values from 10 to 15:

How Hexadecimal to Decimal Conversion Works

Each position in a hexadecimal number represents a power of 16. Starting from the right, the positions represent 160, 161, 162, 163, and so on.

For example, hexadecimal 2F can be converted to decimal as follows:

Hex Digit Power of 16 Value
2 161 32
F 160 15

Therefore: 32 + 15 = 47. So hexadecimal 2F equals decimal 47.

How Decimal to Hexadecimal Conversion Works

To convert a positive decimal whole number to hexadecimal, repeatedly divide the number by 16 and record the remainders. Remainders from 10 through 15 are represented by A through F.

For example, decimal 47 converts to hexadecimal 2F.

Calculation Quotient Remainder
47 ÷ 16 2 15 (F)
2 ÷ 16 0 2

Reading the remainders from bottom to top gives 2F.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is hexadecimal?

Hexadecimal is a base-16 number system that uses the digits 0–9 and the letters A–F.

What is 2F in decimal?

Hexadecimal 2F is equal to decimal 47.

What is 255 in hexadecimal?

Decimal 255 is equal to hexadecimal FF.

Are lowercase hexadecimal letters accepted?

Yes. Hexadecimal values can be written using either uppercase or lowercase letters from A through F.
