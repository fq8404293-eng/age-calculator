What Is an EV Charging Cost Calculator?

An EV Charging Cost Calculator estimates the electricity required to charge an electric vehicle and the approximate cost of that electricity. It can be useful for electric cars, SUVs, vans, motorcycles, and other battery-powered vehicles.

The calculation uses the vehicle's battery capacity, the percentage of the battery being charged, the local electricity price, and an estimated charging efficiency.

Because charging is not perfectly efficient, the amount of electricity drawn from the grid can be greater than the amount of energy actually stored in the battery.

How EV Charging Cost Is Calculated

First, the calculator determines how much battery capacity needs to be added between the starting and target charge levels.

Battery Energy Required

Energy Stored = Battery Capacity × (Target Charge − Starting Charge) ÷ 100

The calculator then accounts for charging losses by dividing the energy stored by the charging efficiency.

Grid Energy Required

Grid Energy = Energy Stored ÷ (Charging Efficiency ÷ 100)

Finally, the estimated grid energy is multiplied by the electricity price.

Charging Cost

Charging Cost = Grid Energy × Electricity Price per kWh

Example EV Charging Cost Calculation

Suppose an electric vehicle has a 60 kWh battery and you want to charge it from 20% to 80%. If electricity costs $0.15 per kWh and charging efficiency is 90%, the calculation works as follows.

What Affects EV Charging Cost?

The cost of charging an EV can vary depending on several factors:

  • Local electricity price
  • Vehicle battery capacity
  • Starting and target battery percentage
  • Charging efficiency
  • Home charging equipment
  • Time-of-use electricity rates
  • Public charging station pricing
  • Taxes, fees, or charging-network charges

Home charging is often priced by the amount of electricity consumed, while public charging stations may use different pricing models, including per-kWh, per-minute, session, or subscription-based charges.

EV Charging Cost FAQ

How much electricity does an EV use when charging?

The amount depends on the battery size, how much the battery is charged, and charging losses. A larger battery or a larger increase in battery percentage generally requires more electricity.

Why is grid electricity higher than battery energy?

Charging systems are not perfectly efficient. Some electricity is lost through the charger, battery management system, wiring, and heat. This means the grid may supply more energy than is ultimately stored in the battery.

Can I use this calculator for an electric motorcycle or scooter?

Yes. The calculation works for any rechargeable electric vehicle when you know its battery capacity and the relevant electricity price.

What electricity price should I enter?

Enter the price you actually pay per kWh. Check your electricity bill for the applicable energy rate. If your bill includes additional charges, your effective cost per kWh may be higher than the basic energy rate.

Does charging from 20% to 80% use 60% of the battery?

The energy stored in the battery is approximately 60% of its stated capacity for a 20% to 80% charge. The electricity drawn from the grid can be higher because of charging losses.

Is EV charging cheaper than gasoline or diesel?

The answer depends on electricity prices, fuel prices, vehicle efficiency, charging costs, and driving conditions. Comparing the energy cost per distance can provide a more useful comparison than looking only at the cost of one charging session.
