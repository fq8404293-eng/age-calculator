Power Formula

A number raised to a power is written as an, where a is the base and n is the exponent.

For example, 23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

What Is an Exponent?

An exponent tells you how many times a number is multiplied by itself. In the expression 53, 5 is the base and 3 is the exponent.

This means 5 is multiplied by itself three times: 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.

Types of Exponents

Positive Exponents

A positive exponent indicates repeated multiplication. For example, 34 means 3 × 3 × 3 × 3, which equals 81.

Zero Exponent

Any nonzero number raised to the power of zero equals 1. Therefore, 70 = 1.

Negative Exponents

A negative exponent represents the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power. For example, 2−3 = 1/23 = 1/8.

Fractional Exponents

A fractional exponent can represent a root. For example, 161/2 equals √16, which is 4.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an exponent?

An exponent indicates how many times a base number is multiplied by itself.

What does a zero exponent mean?

Any nonzero number raised to the power of zero equals 1.

What does a negative exponent mean?

A negative exponent represents a reciprocal. For example, 2−2 equals 1/4.

Can an exponent be a decimal?

Yes. Fractional or decimal exponents can represent roots and other real-number powers when the mathematical result is defined.
