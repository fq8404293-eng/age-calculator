Paint Estimate

How Does the Paint Calculator Work?

The calculator first estimates the total wall area using the room length, width, and wall height. It then subtracts the area of doors and windows that will not be painted.

The remaining paintable area is multiplied by the number of coats. Finally, the total coverage area is divided by the coverage provided by one gallon of paint.

Paint Needed = Paintable Area × Coats ÷ Coverage

How to Calculate Wall Area

For a rectangular room, the total wall area can be calculated using the room perimeter and wall height.

Wall Area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height

For example, a room that is 20 feet long, 15 feet wide, and 8 feet high has:

2 × (20 + 15) × 8 = 560 sq ft

If the room has 40 square feet of doors and windows, the paintable wall area is:

560 − 40 = 520 sq ft

Understanding Paint Coverage

Paint coverage tells you approximately how much surface area one gallon of paint can cover. The actual coverage depends on the specific product and the surface being painted.

Always check the manufacturer's coverage information on the paint container before purchasing paint.

Paint Coverage Meaning
300 sq ft/gal One gallon covers approximately 300 sq ft
350 sq ft/gal One gallon covers approximately 350 sq ft
400 sq ft/gal One gallon covers approximately 400 sq ft

Paint Calculator Example

Suppose a room is 20 feet long, 15 feet wide, and 8 feet high. The total wall area is:

2 × (20 + 15) × 8 = 560 sq ft

If doors and windows account for 40 square feet, the paintable area becomes 520 square feet.

With 2 coats and paint coverage of 350 square feet per gallon:

520 × 2 ÷ 350 ≈ 2.97 gallons

The calculator reports the mathematical estimate and can help you determine an appropriate amount to purchase.

What Can Affect Paint Quantity?

The calculated quantity is an estimate. Actual paint usage can vary depending on the surface and application.

  • Surface texture and porosity
  • Paint type and product coverage
  • Number of coats required
  • Existing wall color
  • Application method
  • Doors, windows, and other unpainted areas

Important Note About Paint Estimates

Paint calculators provide an estimate rather than an exact purchase requirement. Different paints can have different coverage rates, and real-world application can use more or less paint than the theoretical calculation.

For the most accurate estimate, use the coverage information supplied by the paint manufacturer and account for the actual condition of your surfaces.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint do I need for a room?

It depends on the total paintable surface area, number of coats, and paint coverage. Enter these values into the calculator to get an estimate.

Does the calculator subtract doors and windows?

Yes. You can enter the combined area of doors and windows, and that area is subtracted from the total wall area.

Should I include the ceiling?

The main calculation estimates wall paint. If you also want to paint the ceiling, its area can be calculated separately and added to the total paintable area.

How many coats of paint should I use?

The required number of coats depends on the paint product, surface condition, existing color, and desired finish. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for your specific product.

Is the paint estimate exact?

No. It is a mathematical estimate based on the information you provide. Actual paint usage can vary because of surface texture, absorption, application technique, and product coverage.
