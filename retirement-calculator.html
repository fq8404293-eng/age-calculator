What Is Retirement Planning?

Retirement planning is the process of estimating how much money you may need after you stop working and determining how much you may need to save and invest before retirement.

A retirement plan usually considers your current savings, future contributions, investment returns, inflation, expected retirement age, retirement expenses, and other sources of income.

The earlier you begin, the more time your savings have to potentially benefit from compound growth. However, the actual outcome depends on investment performance, contributions, inflation, fees, taxes, and changes in your financial situation.

How the Retirement Calculator Works

The calculator projects your retirement savings from your current age through your planned retirement age.

Your current savings are allowed to grow based on the expected annual return. Monthly contributions are added throughout the projection period and also participate in investment growth.

The calculator then estimates a potential retirement income from the projected savings and adds the Social Security amount you entered.

Inflation is also considered so that you can see how a future retirement income compares with your desired income in today's purchasing power.

Retirement Savings Formula

The future value of existing savings can be represented using compound growth:

Future Value = Current Savings × (1 + r)n

Regular contributions are also accumulated over time. The actual calculation in this tool uses monthly periods so that monthly contributions can be included in the projection.

Here, r represents the periodic investment return and n represents the number of periods.

Why Compound Growth Matters

Compound growth means that investment returns can themselves generate additional returns over time.

For example, if an investment grows from $10,000 to $10,700 during one year at a 7% return, the following year's growth is based on the larger balance if the investment remains invested.

This is one reason time can be an important part of retirement planning. Starting earlier can give savings more years to potentially grow.

Why Inflation Matters

Inflation reduces the purchasing power of money over time.

If prices rise by 3% per year, the same amount of money generally buys fewer goods and services in the future than it does today.

This calculator uses your inflation assumption to help illustrate the difference between a future dollar amount and its approximate purchasing power in today's dollars.

Estimating Retirement Income

Knowing your projected retirement balance is only one part of retirement planning. You also need to consider how that balance could support your spending during retirement.

A common planning approach is to estimate a sustainable withdrawal amount from retirement savings. This calculator uses a simplified withdrawal assumption to provide an educational estimate rather than a personalized withdrawal strategy.

Social Security or other retirement income can provide another source of cash flow. Entering an estimated Social Security amount allows the calculator to show it separately from the estimated investment-based retirement income.

Retirement Calculator Example

Suppose someone is 30 years old, has $25,000 already saved for retirement, contributes $500 per month, and plans to retire at age 65.

If they assume a 7% annual investment return and 3% inflation, the calculator can project how those savings and contributions may grow over the 35-year period.

The result is an estimate, not a guarantee. Actual returns may be higher or lower, contributions may change, and inflation may not remain constant.

Common Retirement Planning Mistakes

  • Waiting too long to begin saving.
  • Underestimating the effect of inflation.
  • Assuming investment returns will be constant every year.
  • Ignoring investment fees and taxes.
  • Failing to increase contributions as income grows.
  • Forgetting healthcare and other retirement expenses.
  • Treating a retirement projection as a guaranteed result.

Important Information

Retirement calculations are highly dependent on the assumptions used. Investment returns are not guaranteed, and actual market performance can vary substantially from year to year.

This calculator does not account for every possible retirement planning factor, including individual tax situations, investment fees, healthcare costs, required minimum distributions, pensions, changing contribution levels, or changes in spending.

The results are intended for educational and planning purposes only. Consider your personal circumstances and consult a qualified financial professional when making important financial decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a retirement calculator estimate?

It estimates how your current savings and future contributions may grow before retirement and provides an approximate retirement income based on the assumptions you enter.

What return should I use?

The appropriate return depends on your investments, asset allocation, fees, time horizon, and market conditions. Testing multiple assumptions can provide a broader view of possible outcomes.

Why is inflation included?

Inflation reduces purchasing power. Including an inflation assumption helps illustrate what future money may be worth in today's purchasing power.

Does the calculator include Social Security?

Yes. You can enter an estimated annual Social Security income. It is included separately from the investment-based retirement income estimate.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. The results are estimates based on the assumptions entered. Investment returns, inflation, expenses, taxes, and personal circumstances can all change.

Is this financial advice?

No. This calculator provides mathematical estimates for educational and planning purposes. It does not provide personalized financial advice.
