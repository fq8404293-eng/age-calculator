What Is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is a regular payment made toward repaying a loan. For a typical reducing-balance loan, each payment contains a portion that goes toward interest and a portion that reduces the outstanding principal.

The exact amount of interest and principal within each payment changes over the life of the loan. Early payments generally contain a larger interest component because the outstanding loan balance is higher. As the principal is reduced, the interest component generally becomes smaller.

This calculator assumes a standard fixed-rate, reducing-balance loan for the basic EMI calculation.

EMI Formula

A commonly used formula for a fixed-rate, reducing-balance loan is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where:

  • P = original loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal
  • n = total number of monthly payments
  • EMI = estimated monthly payment

To convert an annual interest rate into a monthly rate, the annual percentage rate is divided by 12 and then converted from a percentage to a decimal.

How Does an EMI Work?

Imagine a loan of $100,000 with a fixed interest rate. Your monthly payment is calculated so that the loan can be repaid over the selected number of months, assuming the stated rate remains unchanged.

During each month, interest is calculated on the outstanding balance. The rest of the payment reduces the principal. As the outstanding balance becomes smaller, the amount of interest calculated for future payments generally decreases.

This means that the split between principal and interest is not necessarily the same in every payment, even though the scheduled EMI remains constant for a standard fixed-rate loan.

How Loan Tenure Affects EMI

Loan tenure is the length of time you take to repay the loan. It has an important effect on both the monthly payment and the total interest paid.

Loan Tenure Typical Effect on Monthly Payment Typical Effect on Total Interest
Shorter Higher monthly payment Lower total interest, assuming the same rate
Longer Lower monthly payment Higher total interest, assuming the same rate

The exact result depends on the loan amount, interest rate, repayment schedule and other terms.

How Interest Rate Affects EMI

For the same loan amount and tenure, a higher interest rate generally produces a higher monthly payment and increases the total amount of interest paid over the loan.

Even a relatively small difference in the annual interest rate can have a noticeable effect on a long-term loan because interest is calculated repeatedly throughout the repayment period.

When comparing loan offers, look beyond the monthly EMI and consider the total repayment amount and the complete terms of the loan.

Principal vs. Interest

Your EMI is made up of two main components:

  • Principal: the portion that reduces the amount you borrowed.
  • Interest: the cost associated with borrowing the money.

At the beginning of a typical amortizing loan, the outstanding principal is at its highest level. As a result, the interest component can represent a larger portion of the early payments.

As the principal decreases, less interest is calculated on the outstanding balance, allowing a larger portion of the scheduled payment to reduce principal.

Common EMI Calculation Mistakes

Comparing loans only by EMI

A lower monthly payment does not necessarily mean a lower overall borrowing cost. A longer tenure can reduce the EMI while increasing total interest.

Using the wrong interest rate

Make sure the annual rate entered into the calculator matches the rate used for the loan estimate.

Forgetting additional charges

Processing fees, insurance, taxes, penalties and other charges may affect the actual cost of borrowing.

Ignoring variable-rate changes

This calculator assumes the entered rate remains unchanged. A variable-rate loan can produce different payments if the rate changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is a regular payment toward repaying a loan and generally contains both principal and interest.

How is EMI calculated?

For a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance loan, EMI is calculated using the loan principal, monthly interest rate and total number of monthly payments.

What is the EMI formula?

The standard formula is EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the total number of monthly payments.

Does a longer loan tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. Extending the repayment period spreads the loan over more monthly payments, which can reduce the scheduled EMI. However, the total interest can increase.

Does a higher interest rate increase EMI?

Yes. With the same loan amount and repayment period, a higher interest rate generally increases both the monthly payment and total interest.

Can I use this calculator for a home loan?

Yes. It can be used to estimate payments for a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance home loan or another loan with similar repayment mechanics.

Can I use this calculator for a car loan?

Yes. Enter the car loan amount, annual interest rate and repayment period to estimate the monthly payment.

Can I use this calculator for a personal loan?

Yes. The same basic reducing-balance EMI formula can be used for a standard personal loan.

Does this calculator include fees and taxes?

No. The basic calculation does not include processing fees, taxes, insurance, penalties or other additional loan charges.

Why can my lender's EMI be different?

A lender may use different interest calculation methods, payment dates, rounding rules, fees, insurance or other terms. Variable interest rates can also change the actual payment.

Is the EMI guaranteed to stay the same?

The calculator assumes a fixed interest rate. Actual payments may change for variable-rate loans or if other loan terms change.
