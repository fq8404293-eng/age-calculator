How the 2026 Federal Tax Calculator Works

This calculator estimates US federal individual income tax using your annual gross income, filing status, standard deduction, and additional deductions.

Step 1: Select Your Filing Status

Select the filing status that best matches your situation. The available options are Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, and Head of Household.

Step 2: Enter Your Annual Income

Enter your estimated annual gross income before applying deductions.

Step 3: Enter Additional Deductions

If applicable, enter additional deductions that you want included in this simplified estimate.

Step 4: Calculate Taxable Income

The calculator subtracts the applicable standard deduction and additional deductions from gross income.

Taxable Income = Gross Income − Total Deductions

Step 5: Apply Federal Tax Brackets

Federal income tax is calculated progressively. Different portions of taxable income fall into different tax brackets.

This means that reaching a higher marginal tax bracket does not mean that your entire income is taxed at that higher percentage.

Marginal Tax Rate vs. Effective Tax Rate

Marginal Tax Rate

Your marginal tax rate is the percentage applied to the next dollar of taxable income within your applicable federal tax bracket.

For example, being in the 22% federal tax bracket does not mean that all of your taxable income is taxed at 22%.

Effective Tax Rate

Your effective federal tax rate represents your estimated federal income tax as a percentage of your gross income.

Effective Tax Rate = Federal Income Tax ÷ Gross Income × 100

Because the federal income tax system is progressive, the effective tax rate is generally lower than the marginal tax rate for taxpayers with taxable income extending across multiple brackets.

What Is Taxable Income?

Taxable income is the amount of income that remains subject to federal income tax after applicable deductions and adjustments included in the calculation.

This calculator uses a simplified calculation based on gross income, the standard deduction, and the additional deductions entered by the user.

Taxable Income = Gross Income − Total Deductions

If deductions are greater than gross income, taxable income cannot be less than zero for the purposes of this calculator.

How Progressive Federal Tax Brackets Work

The US federal income tax system uses progressive tax brackets. Each bracket applies only to the portion of taxable income that falls within that bracket.

Example

Suppose a taxpayer's taxable income extends into the 22% bracket. The entire taxable income is not automatically taxed at 22%.

Instead, the calculator applies the applicable rate to each portion of taxable income that falls within the corresponding tax bracket.

Important: A higher marginal tax bracket does not mean that all of your income is taxed at that higher rate.
