How the Ratio Calculator Works

A ratio compares two quantities and shows the relationship between them. For example, a ratio of 2:3 means that for every 2 units of the first quantity, there are 3 units of the second quantity.

When the two values have a common factor, the ratio can be simplified. The calculator finds the greatest common divisor of the values and uses it to produce the simplest whole-number ratio whenever possible.

Simplified Ratio = First Value ÷ GCD : Second Value ÷ GCD

Ratio Calculator Example

Suppose there are 12 red objects and 18 blue objects. Their ratio is 12:18.

Step Calculation Result
Original Ratio 12 : 18 12 : 18
Greatest Common Divisor GCD(12, 18) 6
First Part 12 ÷ 6 2
Second Part 18 ÷ 6 3
Simplified Ratio 2 : 3 2 : 3

Therefore, the simplified ratio of 12 to 18 is 2:3.

Understanding Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios describe the same relationship even when their numbers are different. You can create an equivalent ratio by multiplying both parts by the same non-zero number.

Ratio Multiplier Equivalent Ratio
2 : 3 × 2 4 : 6
2 : 3 × 3 6 : 9
2 : 3 × 5 10 : 15

Common Uses of Ratios

Ratios are used in many everyday and mathematical situations, including:

  • Comparing quantities
  • Recipes and ingredient measurements
  • Maps and scale drawings
  • Mixing materials or solutions
  • Comparing financial or business figures
  • Understanding proportions and relationships

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities by showing how much of one quantity there is relative to another. A ratio can be written as 2:3 or as a fraction.

How do you simplify a ratio?

Divide both parts of the ratio by their greatest common divisor. For example, 12:18 becomes 2:3 after dividing both values by 6.

What is an equivalent ratio?

Equivalent ratios have the same relationship between their two parts. Multiplying both parts by the same non-zero number produces an equivalent ratio.

Can ratios contain decimals?

Yes. Ratios can contain decimal values. They can often be converted into whole-number ratios by multiplying both parts by an appropriate factor.
