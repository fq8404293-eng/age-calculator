What Is a Mileage Calculator?

A mileage calculator estimates how efficiently a vehicle uses fuel based on the distance traveled and the amount of fuel consumed. It can be used for cars, motorcycles, scooters, trucks, vans, and other fuel-powered vehicles.

Mileage is commonly expressed as kilometers per liter (km/L) in many countries, while fuel economy in the United States is commonly expressed as miles per US gallon (MPG). Another widely used measurement is liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km).

By entering actual trip distance and fuel usage, you can estimate your vehicle's real-world fuel economy instead of relying only on the manufacturer's published figures.

How to Calculate Mileage

The basic mileage calculation is straightforward: divide the distance traveled by the amount of fuel consumed.

Fuel Economy Formula

Fuel Economy (km/L) = Distance in kilometers ÷ Fuel used in liters

For example, if a vehicle travels 500 km using 33.33 liters of fuel:

500 ÷ 33.33 = 15.00 km/L

For L/100 km, the calculation is reversed because a lower number represents better fuel efficiency:

L/100 km Formula

Fuel Consumption (L/100 km) = Fuel used in liters ÷ Distance in kilometers × 100

US MPG measures how many miles a vehicle can travel using one US gallon of fuel. Higher MPG means better fuel economy.

Understanding Mileage Results

Measurement Meaning Better Result
km/L Kilometers traveled per liter of fuel Higher
L/100 km Liters of fuel used to travel 100 km Lower
US MPG Miles traveled per US gallon Higher

These measurements describe the same basic concept from different perspectives. A vehicle with higher km/L or MPG generally uses less fuel for the same distance. A vehicle with lower L/100 km generally uses less fuel as well.

How to Get an Accurate Real-World Mileage Reading

For a useful real-world mileage measurement, fill the fuel tank to a consistent level and record the vehicle's odometer reading. Drive normally and then refill the tank using the same method.

Record the distance traveled and the amount of fuel added at the next refill. Dividing the distance by the fuel consumed gives an estimate of the vehicle's actual fuel economy.

Repeating this process over several fuel tanks can provide a more representative average because one individual trip can be affected by traffic, weather, road conditions, driving style, load, and air-conditioning use.

What Affects Vehicle Mileage?

Real-world fuel economy can vary considerably depending on how and where a vehicle is driven.

  • Driving speed and acceleration
  • Traffic congestion and frequent stopping
  • Vehicle weight and cargo
  • Tyre pressure and tyre condition
  • Engine maintenance and vehicle condition
  • Road surface and terrain
  • Weather and temperature
  • Use of air conditioning or heating
  • Driving style
  • Fuel quality and vehicle specifications

Because of these factors, the mileage shown on a vehicle's specifications or dashboard may differ from the mileage calculated from actual fuel usage.

Mileage Calculator FAQ

What does km/L mean?

km/L means kilometers per liter. It tells you how many kilometers a vehicle can travel using one liter of fuel. A higher km/L value generally indicates better fuel economy.

What does L/100 km mean?

L/100 km means liters per 100 kilometers. It tells you how much fuel a vehicle uses to travel 100 kilometers. Unlike km/L, a lower L/100 km value generally indicates better efficiency.

What does MPG mean?

MPG means miles per gallon. This calculator uses US gallons when calculating US MPG. A higher MPG value generally means better fuel economy.

Can I calculate motorcycle or scooter mileage?

Yes. The same basic calculation can be used for motorcycles, scooters, cars, and other vehicles. Enter the distance traveled and the amount of fuel consumed.

Why is my actual mileage different from the manufacturer's figure?

Published fuel-economy figures are measured under specified testing conditions. Real-world mileage can vary because of traffic, speed, driving style, road conditions, vehicle load, weather, maintenance, and other factors.

Which is better, higher or lower mileage?

For km/L and MPG, a higher number generally indicates better fuel economy. For L/100 km, a lower number indicates better efficiency because it means less fuel is required to travel the same distance.
